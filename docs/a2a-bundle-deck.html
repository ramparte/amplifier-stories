The Problem

Your agent is brilliant — but it’s alone

🧠
Powerful Locally
Your Amplifier agent can search the web, write code, manage files, reason through hard problems. It’s your most capable assistant.
🚧
Isolated by Default
But it can’t talk to anyone else’s agent. No way to send a question, request, or task to another person’s Amplifier session.
💡
What If?
What if your research agent could ask your code-review agent about a codebase — or ask your spouse’s agent about dinner plans?
Multi-instance: “Hey research agent, ask my code review agent what changed in the auth module this week.”
Multi-person: “Hey agent, ask Sarah’s agent where she wants to go for dinner tonight.”
The Solution

amplifier-bundle-a2a lets sessions discover, message, and collaborate

A standalone bundle implementing Google’s Agent-to-Agent protocol — two Amplifier sessions connect over direct peer-to-peer HTTP, no cloud relay.

Trust & Security

Nothing reaches your agent until you approve it — like Bluetooth pairing

Every sender lands in one of three tiers, and unknown agents are blocked by default.

Trusted
Full access to your agent’s tools. Gets autonomous replies. For close contacts you fully trust.
Known
Read-only. Can message you and get human-approved replies. No tool access. For work contacts.
Unknown
Blocked by default. Can’t reach you until you explicitly approve them. No surprises.
🔐  Per-contact scoping
Each contact gets its own trust level, mode, and tool access. Fine-grained control over every relationship.
🔗  First-contact approval
A new agent shows you its Agent Card; you approve, reject, or ignore. Share URLs to pair with people you know.
Interaction Modes

Your trust level picks how each message is answered

One of three modes fires per contact — an escalation ladder from hands-free to fully hands-on.

C
Autonomous
Trusted contacts get instant answers. Your agent uses its full toolkit — no human in the loop.
A
Notify
Messages are queued. Your agent notifies you and you write the reply. The sender waits.
B
Live
Messages inject into your active session in real time. You see them arrive and respond on the spot.

Early Access

Honest about where this is: the protocol is solid, the reach is early

What works today
  • 290 tests passing — full protocol coverage
  • Localhost — multiple instances, same machine
  • LAN / office network — same subnet, via mDNS
  • Tailscale / VPN — anywhere ports are reachable
  • Three modes + trust management — per-contact controls
What's not there yet
  • No relay service — no NAT traversal or cloud proxy
  • No encryption — relies on network-level trust (VPN, LAN)
  • Direct port access required — firewalls block this
  • No public internet — can't reach agents behind NAT
  • Early WIP — the API surface may change

Think Bluetooth in the early days — powerful up close, with clear limits on range. The reach grows from here.
