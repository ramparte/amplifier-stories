Meet the bundle

The Design Intelligence bundle already watched design — every week, automatically

Before any of this, Design Intelligence Enhanced was already an impressive pipeline: it scraped design showcase sites on a weekly schedule, ran vision AI over the screenshots, and piled the results into a growing archive of trend data.

It never stopped collecting. That is exactly what makes the next question sting — because collecting is not the same as learning.

The gap

It got more informed every week — and never one bit smarter

Someone asked the obvious question. The honest answer was uncomfortable.

"How does it actually self-improve?"
"It gets more informed over time, but it doesn't get smarter over time."
— End-of-session reflection

The insight

Turning data into taste took two layers, not one

The single claim of the design: self-improvement isn't one monolithic "learning engine." It's two complementary layers, each meaningless without the other — and together they close the loop.

Layer B — Personal Taste Profile
Remembers what you like
Per-user and per-project preferences that evolve through inferred-then-confirmed suggestions. The system watches your choices, notices patterns, proposes updates — but nothing writes without your say.
built on top of
Layer A — Collective Design Intelligence
Knows what's happening in design
The knowledge base grows organically from real project work. When agents hit archive patterns that aren't captured yet, they propose additions inline.

B without A is a static preference file. A without B is a knowledge base nobody's taste is mapped against. Together, they learn.

The payoff

A passive archive became a system that learns your taste — taught, not coded

Same pipeline, same data. What changed was the judgment wrapped around it — delivered entirely as instructions.

How it surfaces

Now every agent reasons in three modes: decide, flag, recommend

Taste isn't a sidebar or a notification — it's woven into how each agent thinks, and confidence decides which voice it uses.

Decide
"Using a muted earth-tone palette here — your profile strongly favors it and it fits this project's editorial tone."

High confidence, context aligns: the agent applies the preference and explains why. Transparent, not blocking.
Flag
"Your profile leans toward generous whitespace, but this dashboard has 12 data points to keep scannable. Tighter grouping, or whitespace with progressive disclosure?"

Tension between preference and context: the agent asks for a call.
Recommend
"You favor serif headlines, and there's a strong trend toward condensed bold sans for data-dense headers. Worth considering where you need density?"

An archive trend meets a preference: the agent suggests evolution.
The turn

None of it was specced — the whole design came from a 20-minute conversation

The architecture emerged through structured brainstorming: five questions, five answers, the system presenting options and the user making every call. A pattern showed up in those calls — the user kept choosing the nuanced "both, layered" option.

"Every time you give me a 'both' option I want to select both. It really is layered, and there are per-project considerations."

That instinct shaped everything: layered profiles, not flat; inferred-then-confirmed, not silent or manual; woven into reasoning, not bolted on. The machine got its taste from a human's judgment — then subagents built it straight from the plan.

What it means

Instructions are infrastructure — design the loop, not the data store

01
Instructions are infrastructure
185 lines of well-structured markdown gave 8 agents a new capability. In the Amplifier ecosystem, behavior is composition — you teach agents by writing clear instructions, not by writing code.
02
Design the loop, not the data store
The bundle already collected data. What it lacked was the loop: observe, propose, confirm, remember. Self-improvement wasn't about more data — it was about closing the feedback cycle, with user agency at every write.

Brainstorm before you build: twenty minutes of options-and-decisions produced a design that a document-first approach would have taken days to spec.
