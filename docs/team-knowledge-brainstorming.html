The goal & the reframe

One question changed the whole project

“I need to build an eval system that measures whether team-knowledge actually helps people work.”

Clear goal, specific scope. But instead of designing from zero, the developer ran three targeted searches against team_knowledge — the team's shared, searchable index of everyone's work. That flipped the question:

“How do I build an eval system?”

“Which of my teammates' work do I compose?”

What search surfaced

Teammates had already built the hard parts

The single claim of this beat: the three pieces an eval system needs already existed, spread across three people's repos.

Why this matters

Three teammates' prior work was leveraged into a new design — no duplication, no wasted effort. When a team's knowledge is searchable, every investment in tooling pays dividends beyond the original project.

Build something good and your teammates will build on it — even when you don't know it's happening. You don't need to memorize everyone's repos. The knowledge base knows them for you.

The recursive proof
