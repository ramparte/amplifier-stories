The Payoff

Your voice is in the data —
and SamVoice writes in it

SamVoice turns the measured fingerprint into working tools. A frontier model is calibrated to your exact patterns, then every draft is scored against them — and a fine-tuned classifier proves the signal is real.

Three commands, one voice

  • write — generate a full draft with voice-matched exemplars
  • coach — paragraph-by-paragraph scoring with fix suggestions
  • find — retrieve your best corpus paragraphs for any topic

The signal is provable

A ModernBERT classifier, trained on 6,724 labeled examples, distinguishes voice-matched from AI-generic text at 100% accuracy on the held-out test set. The fingerprint isn’t a metaphor — it’s a decision boundary.

The System

Two tracks to the same voice:
prompt it, or fine-tune it

Voice Prompt + Retrieval

No GPU needed. A data-derived voice prompt encodes the punctuation fingerprint, vocabulary, and anti-patterns; a retrieval tool pulls topic-relevant exemplar paragraphs to calibrate the frontier model. Recommended for daily use.

LoRA Fine-Tuned Models

GPU required. Two LoRA adapters on Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct, trained on the real corpus: one scores text (6,724 examples), one rewrites AI-generic into voice-matched (907 pairs). Built for batch processing and research.

Same destination, different cost: the voice prompt runs anywhere; the LoRA models score entire books paragraph by paragraph.

Workflow

From topic to voice-matched draft
in four steps

1

Index the Corpus

Load all 226 essays (~1,700 paragraphs). Score each on voice dimensions: informal quantifiers, hedging, parentheticals, scare quotes, transitions, sentence variety.

2

Retrieve Exemplars

For a topic, find paragraphs ranked by (relevance × voice quality). Backfill with highest-voice-score paragraphs when topical matches run short.

3

Build the Voice Prompt

Combine the quantitative fingerprint with structural rules, anti-patterns, and the retrieved exemplars into one calibrated system prompt.

4

Generate & Coach

Send to the model, then score the result paragraph-by-paragraph — flagging formal transitions, AI slop, missing uncontracted forms, semicolons.

Training Innovation

The model learns your voice
by learning what it is not

Hard negatives: real author paragraphs with exactly one voice dimension violated. Each teaches the classifier a precise boundary — 10 violation types, 300+ examples, calibrated labels from 0.25 to 0.55.

formal_transitions

“But” → “However” / “Moreover” — the fastest tell of AI text.

semicolonitis

Adds semicolons, which sit near zero in the real corpus (0.2/1K).

ai_slop_injection

Injects “transformative,” “it’s worth noting” — the generic vocabulary.

Other types strip parentheticals, remove scare quotes, over-contract, or flatten hedging — each a specific way a voice drifts.

The Bigger Picture

Package the fingerprint — and
any voice becomes portable

A voice pack is the contract between general-purpose writing tools and one specific voice. A person, a character, a brand — each is just a pack of a fingerprint, a rubric, anti-patterns, and exemplars.

Pipelines are generic

Judge, rewrite, prune, densify — the pipeline logic never changes. It works with any voice pack you hand it.

Voice packs are specific

What “good” sounds like, what to avoid, how to score — one pack per voice, portable across every tool. All 7,769 lines of Python stay the same.

A pack is four files: voice-prompt.md (the fingerprint), rubric-anchors.yaml (6 scoring dimensions), anti-patterns.yaml, and exemplars/ (ground-truth samples).
