The Blind Spot

Coding is solved.
Nobody grades how you build.

AI can write the code. What matters now is how you choose what to build, how efficiently you direct it, whether you know when it’s good, how fast your ambition scales, and whether anyone else can see it. Five dimensions mark the shift:

  • Problem Selection
  • Leverage Ratio
  • Taste
  • Ambition Trajectory
  • Visibility
You can't improve what you can't see.
Every Amplifier session records how you build — and nobody was reading it.
The Point

Say “Coach me” — get how
you build, reflected back.

Builder Coach turns your unread session history into a mirror.

Plenty of tools tell you whether your code is good. Builder Coach is the one that tells you whether you’re getting better at building — grounded in what you actually did, session by session, with a claim for every observation.

“Coach me” — that's the entire trigger.
The Pipeline

Two words trigger a
three-step evidence pipeline.

1

Gather Evidence

Explorer scans session history in an adaptive window — starts at 7 days and auto-expands (7 → 14 → 28 → 90) until it has enough. Raw data becomes per-dimension observations.

2

Evaluate Against Framework

The coaching agent maps evidence to the five dimensions and assigns qualitative levels — Low through Exceptional. Every claim must cite a specific session or pattern.

3

Write the Snapshot

The storyteller compresses the evaluation into a scannable snapshot — 300-word ceiling, second person, no numeric scores. Stateless: each one stands alone.

Design Principles

It calibrates to what

Tone Calibration

Under 10 sessions: tentative and curious. 10–100: observational and grounded. 100+: confident and direct. The system knows what it doesn’t know.

Never Refuses to Coach

The evidence window defaults to 7 days and expands automatically — 14, 28, then 90 — always evaluating with whatever is available.

Composable in One Line

Coaching ships as standalone YAML that pulls in agents and context. No code changes to add it to any bundle.

The Journey

The coach couldn't coach
its own builder.

When the author of Builder Coach said “Coach me” to their own system, nothing happened. The phrase wasn’t in the trigger list. The namespace paths were broken. Competing delegation examples confused the routing. Three root causes — all found by trying to use the thing you just built.

The format had its own reckoning. The first version produced long narrative essays; nobody wants a character study at 8 AM. Five iterations compressed it to snapshot, strengths, gaps, action, scorecard — under 300 words. The question builders actually have is “Am I doing well?”, not “Tell me my story.”

The irony isn’t a bug — it’s the point. A tool that watches how you work will, inevitably, catch you in the act of not watching yourself.
